# Communicating the Work

Working notes for a series of guides, blog posts, and social media about SLTs as the interface for human-agent collaboration.

## Rough Draft: Personal Thinking

In 2020, when I started talking about Student Learning Targets, Project Based Learning, and student-centered education, I tried to describe my work to new collaborators as building student "agency". The word did not make sense to people at that time.

Things are different now in 2026. Now that we have agents and agentic systems, it is natural to be talking more about "agency". Compare to other times that new technology leads to new language for us humans to describe our place in the work (industrial revolution, 20th century mass-production, 21st century internet economy) — at each stage, we use technical language to describe the state of being human.

As a teacher, I'd like to name some concepts that were helpful to teachers like me, who were focused on student-centered learning with one goal in mind: increasing student agency. It's a goal that always makes sense to people inside the system, and yet has been hard to describe to people outside of education.

AI presents the opportunity to think about how any of us learn to do things, and then to maintain and use that knowledge to keep doing things.

Andamio has always been about agency. From the start of my journey learning about blockchain and wondering about the long term conversations that blockchain and education might have, I have seen in blockchain the chance to increase individual agency in ways that map to what I was trying to do in the classroom. I could not have predicted how AI would inform these conversations. But now it's all coming together.

It has been clear to me for a long time that Student Learning Targets are the interface for collaboration, but the idea was not yet mature. AI provides context for maturing this idea.

## Draft Tweets

**1.**
In 2020 I kept telling people I was building "student agency." Nobody outside education knew what I meant. In 2026, everyone is talking about "agents." Same word. Same root concept. Different context that finally makes it land.

**2.**
Every major technology shift gives us new language to describe what it means to be human. The industrial revolution gave us "productivity." The internet gave us "networks." AI is giving us "agency" — and teachers have been working on that problem for decades.

**3.**
The best framework I've found for working with AI agents comes from education research, not computer science. Student Learning Targets — specific, assessable capability claims — work as well for delegating to an AI as they do for coaching a 10th grader. The loop is the same.

**4.**
A Student Learning Target is a falsifiable claim: "I can build a CLI with multiple commands." It doesn't matter whether the claimant is a student or an AI agent. What matters is: can the work be produced, and can someone qualified verify it meets the target?

**5.**
Teachers have a technique called Fist to Five. Students hold up 0–5 fingers to signal confidence against a learning target. We adapted it for AI agents. The result: a real-time signal that tells you exactly where to trust the AI and where to step in. Education solved this problem first.

**6.**
The biggest mistake in working with AI is asking "Can the AI do this?" That's like asking "Can a person do this?" in a classroom. The answer depends on which person, with what preparation. For AI: which skill, with what context. The skill is the student. Everything else is the classroom.

**7.**
Blockchain gave us a way to verify credentials. AI gave us a way to assess capability in real time. Student Learning Targets connect the two: a credential is a set of SLTs, and each SLT can be assessed for both human mastery and agent readiness. The credential becomes a delegation map.

**8.**
Here is the honest version of "trust the AI": some tasks it handles end-to-end, some it handles with review, some it cannot do at all. The distribution is measurable. The goal is not to trust everything. The goal is to know exactly where trust is warranted and route work accordingly.

**9.**
The way you make an AI agent better at something is structurally identical to how a student learns: define the target, attempt the work, diagnose the gap, close the gap, try again. The mechanism is different — students internalize, agents receive context — but the loop is the same.

**10.**
AI does not replace agency. It redefines what agency requires. For students: understanding what you're learning and why. For agents: having the right context and knowing when you don't. For humans working with both: the skill of writing targets clear enough that anyone — human or machine — can aim at them.
